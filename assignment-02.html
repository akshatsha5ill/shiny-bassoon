SECTION A: Short Definitions

1. Stainless Steel Rust Resistance

Chromium is the main element added to steel to make it stainless. It forms a thin protective oxide layer on the surface that prevents rusting and protects the metal from corrosion.

2. Specific Strength

Specific strength is the ratio of a material's strength to its density (weight per unit volume). High specific strength is important for mine shaft cage hoist cables because it allows them to carry heavy loads while keeping their own weight low.

3. Composite Materials

A composite material is made by combining two or more different meterials to obtain better properties. For example, fiberglass is a composite made of glass fibers and a plastic resin, providing good strength with low weight.

4. Smart Materials

A Shape Memory Alloy (SMA) is a smart material that can return to its original shape when heated after being deformed. This property makes it useful in automatic actuators, sensors, and temprature-controlled devices.

5. Scrap Metal Recycling

Recycling old steel mining machinary machinery is better for the environment because it reduces the need for mining fresh iron ore. It also saves energy, reduces waste, and lowers pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

SECTION B: Short Concept Questions

1. Stainless Steel in Slurry Pipes

Austenitic stainless steel, such as grades 304 and 316, is used in mine processing plants because it offers better corrosion resistance than plain carbon steel. It can withstand moisture and many acidic or corrosive conditions found in slurry pipes. Grade 316 contains molybdenum, which improves its resistance to pitting and crevice corrosion, especially in chloride-containing environments. Although stainless steel costs more initially, its longer service life can reduce maintanance and replacement costs.

2. Aluminium vs. Titanium

Aluminium and titanium alloys are both used to manufacture lightweight mining tools, but they have different properties.

Property Aluminium Alloys Titanium Alloys
Weight Very light Light, but heavier than aluminium
Strength Moderate to high High
Corrosion resistance Good Excellent
Cost Relatively low Very expensive
Applications Tool bodies, frames, and covers High-strength, corrosion-resistant components

Conclusion: Aluminium is suitable for low-cost, lightweight mining tools. Titanium is better for applications requiring high strength and excellent corrosion resistance, but its high cost limits its use.

3. Fiber Composites (FRP)

Fiber-Reinforced Polymer (FRP) is a composite material made of strong fibers and a resin matrix.

  • Role of fibers: The fibers provide strength and stiffness and carry most of the applied load.
  • Role of resin matrix: The resin holds the fibers together, protects them from moisture and damage, and transfers loads between the fibers.

For example, glass fibers provide strength, while epoxy resin binds them together to form a strong and lightweight composite rod.

4. Mine Safety Sensors

Piezoelectric materials generate an electrical charge when mechanical pressure or stress is applied to them. These materials can be used in sensors installed on tunnel walls to detect changes in rock stress or vibration. When the measured signals exceed a preset safety limit, the monitoring system can send an alarm to warn miners about possible rock instability. Such sensors support mine safety, but they must be calibarated calibrated and used with other monitoring methods.

5. Benefits and Challenges of Recycling

Two main benefits:

  1. Recycling scrap steel reduces the need for fresh iron ore mining and saves natural resorces resources.
  2. It can reduce energy consumption, waste, and environmental pollution compared with producing steel entirely from raw materials.

Two main challenges:

  1. Sorting old machine parts is difficult because they may contain different metals, alloys, coatings, and contaminants.
  2. Scrap parts may be damaged, corroded, or mixed with non-metallic materials, making cleaning and processing more expensive.

SECTION C: Material Selection for Mine Safety

Engineering Task: Miner Safety Helmet Design

The objective is to select the best material for manufacturing protective safety helmets for underground coal miners. The helmet must protect miners from falling rocks, remain comfortable during long working hours, resist moisture and mild acids, and have a reasonable production cost.

Task 1: Requirements

The four key properties required for a miner's safety helmet material are:

  1. High impact resistance: The material must absorb impact energy and protect the miner's head from falling rocks and other objects.
  2. Low weight: The helmet should be lightweight and comfortable to wear during an eight-hour shift.
  3. Corrosion and chemical resistance: The material should resist moisture, mild acids, and other environmental conditions found in underground mines.
  4. Low production cost: The material should be affordable to manufacture and maintain while providing reliable protection.

Task 2: Material Selection

The best material option from the given comparison table is High-Density Polymer Composite.

According to the table, it has high impact resistance, very low weight, excellent corrosion resistance, and a moderate cost. These properties make it the most suitable option among the three listed materials for a mining safety helmet.

Task 3: Justification

  1. High-Density Polymer Composite vs. Cast Iron
    High-Density Polymer Composite is better than cast iron because it is lightweight and has high impact resistance. Cast iron is very heavy and brittle, so it may crack under sudden impact. Therefore, the polymer composite is more suitable for a helmet worn by miners.
  2. High-Density Polymer Composite vs. Heavy Structural Steel
    High-Density Polymer Composite is lighter than heavy structural steel, making it more comfortable for miners during long working hours. It also has excellent corrosion resistance, whereas structural steel can rust in moist underground conditions. Thus, the polymer composite requires less protection against corrosion.
  3. Overall Justification
    High-Density Polymer Composite provides a good combination of impact resistance, low weight, corrosion resistance, and reasonable cost. It is therefore the best choice among the given options for manufacturing miner safety helmets. However, the finished helmet must also pass the applicable safety and impact tests before being used in an actual mine.

Conclusion

Advanced materials play an important role in improving the safety, efficiency, and durability of mining equipment. Stainless steel provides corrosion resistance, composites offer high strength with low weight, and smart materials support sensing and monitoring applications. Recycling scrap metals also helps conserve natural resources and reduce environmental pollution. For the given helmet selection task, High-Density Polymer Composite is the most suitable option because it combines impact resistance, lightweight construction, corrosion resistance, and reasonable cost.
